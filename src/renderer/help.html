Rest the pointer on any tool to see what it does. A greyed-out tool cannot work on the page in front — clicking it says why. Press and hold a tool, then slide it sideways, to move it to another place on the toolbar (Alt+Shift+← / → moves the focused one); Settings → Appearance puts them back.

The ☰ menu at the end of the toolbar has every tool and command, so the toolbar only needs the ones you use all the time: new tabs and windows, every tool, extensions, passwords, history and recently closed tabs, bookmarks, downloads, zoom and full screen, print, find, saving the page, and more. A tool opened from the menu works exactly as from its button. The pin beside a tool puts its button on the toolbar, or takes it off.

The omnibox

Type anything. A hostname goes straight there; anything else goes to your search engine — DuckDuckGo unless you changed it in Settings → Search engine, where there are ten to pick from. A new tab puts the caret here already, so you can just start typing.

Resize Tabs shrink as you open more: full, then close-button-only on the active tab, then favicon only. Past that the strip scrolls and keeps the active tab in view. —

Pinned tabs and groups

Right-click any tab to pin it. Pinned tabs shrink to their favicon, sit at the front of the strip, and come back when the browser is reopened — even when it is set to start with a new tab.

The same menu files tabs into a group. A group is a coloured, named run of adjacent tabs; click its chip to rename it, recolour it, add a tab to it, ungroup it or close it in one go. Dragging a tab in among a group's tabs joins it; dragging it clear of them leaves.

Ctrl-click tabs to select several at once, or shift-click to take the run between two. Whatever the tab menu does next, it does to all of them.

Shift+Alt+C new tab in the current group · Shift+Alt+W close the current group

Sleeping tabs

A tab left alone for a while has its page thrown away and its memory handed back. The tab stays where it is, marked with a ring around its favicon, and reloads when you return to it.

Its Back and Forward history comes back with it — the whole list, at the same position. The page is genuinely reloaded, so it returns scrolled to the top rather than where you left it.

The tab you are looking at, its split-view companion and anything playing sound are never touched. Turn it off, or change the delay, in Settings → Performance.

Bookmarks

The bookmarks bar under the toolbar shows what is filed in it, folders first — click a folder for its contents, middle- or Ctrl+click anything to open it in a tab behind this one. Everything else is behind All bookmarks. Turn the bar off in Settings → Appearance.

The bookmarks button opens a sidebar beside the page — the page gets narrower rather than covered. Drag a bookmark onto a folder to move it, or a folder onto another to nest it. Hover a row for rename and delete; deleting a folder can keep its bookmarks. Export… writes a bookmarks file every other browser can import. Drag the sidebar's left edge to make it wider or narrower; it keeps that width.

Passwords

Sign in somewhere and IE2 offers to save the login; change the password and it offers to update it. The first time, it asks you to choose a master password. Everything saved — usernames and notes as well as passwords — is encrypted with it (scrypt and AES-256-GCM). Only which sites have a login is stored in the clear.

The vault locks itself after a few idle minutes (set it on the passwords page). There is no way to recover a forgotten master password: the only way forward is deleting the vault. Amnesia tabs never offer to save. Exporting to CSV asks for the master password again; the file it writes is plain text, so delete it once it is imported elsewhere. Logins from Chrome, Edge, Brave or Firefox come in the same way: export a CSV there, then Import CSV… here.

Sound

Audio effects (the waveform button) change whatever the tab in front is playing: speed, reverb, room size and bass boost, and Keep original pitch — off is the classic slowed, deeper sound. Slowed + Reverb and Nightcore are one click away; Save as preset keeps your own mix. The tab keeps its effects from song to song, and Remember for this site brings them back whenever the site opens. Double-click a value to reset just that one. Installed apps have the same button in their title bar.

Protected players — Spotify on the web, Netflix — get the speed change but not reverb or bass. Their sound is locked away from the page, and the panel says so.

Mood (the cloud) plays rain, wind, thunder and crickets, all generated by the browser, behind whatever you are reading. One slider per sound, presets, Animate to let the mix drift by itself, a thunder-frequency slider, and a sleep timer that fades out. It keeps playing with the panel closed; the cloud stays lit while it does.

Installed apps

Sites that offer it — YouTube Music, Spotify and the like — show an install button in the address bar. Installing puts a shortcut on your desktop with the site's own icon. It opens in a window of its own, with its own taskbar button, whether or not IE2 is already open; opening it again brings that window forward.

The app's title bar has back, reload, the audio effects, your extensions, and a button that opens the current page in a normal tab. Ctrl+R reloads, Alt+← and Alt+→ go back and forward, Ctrl+W closes the window. Links to other sites open in the browser. Apps share the browser's session, so you stay signed in, saved passwords fill in, and ads are still blocked.

Extensions

IE2 runs Chrome extensions. Open one in the Chrome Web Store and press its Add button; IE2 says what it will be able to do, and anything it needs that IE2 does not have, before it is added. Extensions update themselves.

The jigsaw button lists them. Click one to open it, the pin to keep it beside the address bar, and ⋯ for its options, to turn it off, or to remove it. Right-clicking a pinned one does the same. Their right-click items appear on pages, above Inspect, and their numbers show on their buttons.

Extensions work in normal tabs and installed apps, never in amnesia tabs. A few kinds of extension cannot work here: VPN extensions, which need Chrome's proxy settings, and ones built on Chrome's side panel or screen recording.

Dark mode

The moon button makes websites dark. It happens as the page is drawn, not after, so a white page never flashes up first. Sites with a dark theme of their own show that; the rest are darkened by the browser engine itself, which leaves photos and video alone and leaves already-dark pages as they are.

If a site looks wrong, switch it off for that site in the same panel. It changes at once, without reloading.

What gets written down

Everything lives in one SQLite file on your machine. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, because there is nowhere to upload it to.

This page and the new tab page never never n/a

Honest limitation: the login/checkout exclusion is pattern matching on hostnames and paths — accounts.*, /login, /checkout and friends. A logged-in dashboard on an ordinary URL will have its text captured. Use an amnesia tab, or add the domain to the never-remember list in Settings → Memory. Camera, microphone and location are denied to every site with no prompt, because the prompt UI does not exist yet.

Right-clicking a page

Links offer open in new tab, open in amnesia tab, open in new window and copy link address. Images add open, copy address and save. Selected text offers copy and a search for it. Anywhere else gives back, forward, reload and inspect.

Settings

Get started What opens on startup, and which page counts as home.
Search DuckDuckGo, Google, Brave, Bing, Yandex, Yahoo, Startpage, Ecosia, Mojeek or Wikipedia. Omnibox suggestions can be switched off entirely.
Appearance Seven accent colours, tab width, animations on or off, website scrollbars restyled to match the browser, and which toolbar buttons are shown.
Memory Turn history and text capture off, decide whether amnesia tabs count blocked requests, see what is stored, and manage the never-remember list — including wiping one site's history on demand.
Privacy Forget everything, with an option to clear cookies and sign you out of sites at the same time. Bookmarks are always kept.

Internal pages

ie2://home New tab page: recall search over everything you have read, statistics about your reading habits, and recent visits.
ie2://history Everywhere you have been, by day. Remove single pages, a day at a time, or the lot — and unlike Forget everything, the text of those pages is kept, so recall still finds them.
ie2://bookmarks Everything saved, in folders, including anything imported — the same page the bookmarks sidebar shows.
ie2://passwords Saved logins: add, edit, show, copy, delete, generate, import and export. Opens locked; the master password unlocks it.
ie2://settings Everything above, including your own theme colours. Reachable from the ⚙ button.
ie2://help This manual.
